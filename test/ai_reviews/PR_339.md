## Findings

No P1 or P2 findings. Points a reviewer should check:

- Low, by design: sampling uses its own schedule (a discrete callback whose
  sample times are registered as solver stops) rather than
  `PeriodicCallback`. While writing the tests, a navigation
  `PeriodicCallback` did not tick at the run's final time, and a 7c test
  showed a control tick lost at a checkpoint-segment boundary; the sensor
  schedule samples at the run's start, at every multiple of the period and
  at the final time, the same in a checkpointed run. The `PeriodicCallback`
  behaviour is raised separately.
- Low, by design: each sample is a solver stop, so a run with fast sensors
  takes more steps; with sensors the dynamics agree with a run without them
  to the solver's tolerance, not bit for bit.
- Low, by design: the sample callback clears the derivative-discontinuity
  flag (sensors only observe). DiffEqBase 7 captures each discrete
  callback's verdict after its `affect!` and folds them with OR, so the
  clearing cannot hide another callback's discontinuity in the same step.
- Low, modelling: no latency, quantization or saturation; the star tracker
  has no exclusion angles; the magnetometer's field is the torque-rod field
  model (Earth only).
- Low: the field model reads the time from the shared buffer the
  right-hand side keeps; the sample sets it to the sample's time and
  restores it.
- Low: user kinds keep their run state in five fields the engine fills
  (`period_s`, `seed`, `rng`, `latest`, `latest_time_s`); `add_sensor!`
  refuses a kind without them or one that is not mutable.
