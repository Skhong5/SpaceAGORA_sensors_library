## Findings

No P1 or P2 findings. Points a reviewer should check:

- Low, by design: the lever arm is measured from the body origin, the
  centre of mass of the dynamics (as for the thrusters' torques); the
  angular acceleration is the solver's derivative at the accepted step, like
  the specific force (the value before a switch at a callback-driven step).
- Low, modelling: the albedo model takes the reflected light from the
  planet's centre (the lit cap's centroid lies toward the subsolar point),
  uses a uniform albedo and the far-field Lambert sphere, which is rougher in
  low orbit; the planet's disk counts as in view when its edge is within the
  field of view; eclipse still gives no reading.
- Low: the GNSS altitude is the planet-frame sample's (the engine's
  altitude over the planet); above `max_altitude_m` the receiver reports
  NaN, with no reacquisition delay.
- Low: an accelerometer mounted on a link away from the body origin now
  reads the rotation terms (before, every accelerometer read the centre of
  mass's specific force); on the root link at the origin, with no
  `position_m`, the reading is the same arithmetic as before.

## P1 Assessment

No P1 findings. With the defaults (no `position_m` off the origin, the
inertial frame without a cutoff, no albedo) every reading takes the previous
arithmetic, and the four fingerprints are bitwise unchanged against main.

## Tests Added/Updated

- `test/unit/vehicle/sensor_tests.jl`: an accelerometer 1 m from the centre of mass reads `ω × (ω × r)` in a torque-free spin, and `ω̇ × r` plus the centripetal term while a wheel spins the body up; a planet-fixed GNSS fix keeps the inertial position's norm and differs from it by the planet's rotation, and receivers above and below their coverage read NaN and the inertial fix; the frame keyword is validated; a sun sensor with albedo leans toward the planet by the Lambertian weight at a 90° phase angle, reads the Sun alone over the night side or with the planet out of view, and the ten-argument `SensorTruth` leaves the radius NaN.
